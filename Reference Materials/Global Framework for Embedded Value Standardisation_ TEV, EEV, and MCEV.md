### Global Framework for Embedded Value Standardisation: TEV, EEV, and MCEV

#### 1\. Executive Foundations of Embedded Value (EV)

Embedded Value (EV) is a sophisticated actuarial construct designed to measure the consolidated value of shareholder interests in a life insurance company. Moving beyond traditional book value, EV provides a snapshot of economic value by capturing the present value of future profits generated by the existing book of business. For the Chief Actuary and corporate strategist, EV is an indispensable metric for performance measurement, capital allocation, and investor relations. It is particularly critical in mergers and acquisitions (M\&A), as it provides a baseline for the "in-force" value that is non-negotiable in transaction pricing.The fundamental value under this framework is defined as:  $$EV \= PVFP \+ ANAV$$   *(Or*  *$EV \= ANW \+ VIF*$  *)*EV is viewed as a "conservative" measure because it focuses exclusively on value already created. It ignores potential value from future new business and excludes intangible goodwill. When a valuation extends to include the discounted value of future sales capacity, it is termed the  **Appraisal Value** . Consequently, for mature insurers, EV constitutes a high proportion of the Appraisal Value, whereas for smaller, high-growth entities, the EV may represent only a fraction of the total economic worth.

#### 2\. Mechanics of Adjusted Net Worth (ANW)

Adjusted Net Worth (ANW) represents the "realisable value" of capital and surplus. While the Value In-Force (VIF) looks forward, the ANW reflects accumulated past profits. It is defined as the tangible shareholder wealth that could theoretically be distributed as dividends, adjusted for assets that are "locked in" to support the business.ANW is decomposed into:

1. **Required Capital (RC):**  The capital explicitly allocated to meet regulatory requirements or internal financial strength targets. It is important to note that the RC reported in EV may fall short of a company's internal "Own Risk Assessment" or specific capital management policies.  
2. **Free Surplus (FS):**  Capital held in excess of RC that is immediately available for distribution.To ensure the ANW reflects actual market value, statutory capital must be adjusted. This process explicitly excludes intangible assets like GAAP goodwill. This adjustment is strategic: because such intangibles have no "realisable value," they cannot be readily converted into shareholder dividends.There are two primary approaches for marking assets to market:  
* **Mark-to-Market and Tax-Effected:**  All invested assets supporting the ANW are adjusted to market value, assuming a notional sale and accounting for the associated tax impact.  
* **Bifurcated Approach:**  Only assets supporting the Free Surplus are marked to market. Assets supporting Required Capital remain at book value, with the cost of capital subsequently based on book value rates of return.The capital held within the ANW directly generates the "opportunity costs" that must be subtracted in the VIF calculation.

#### 3\. The Actuarial Mechanics of Value In-Force (VIF)

Value In-Force (VIF) represents the "future profit engine" of the insurer. It is the present value of future shareholder cash flows expected to emerge as the current portfolio runs off. The precise formula is:  $$VIF \= PVFP \- CoC$$   *(Present Value of Future Profits minus the Cost of Capital)*

##### The Projection of Statutory Book Profit ( $BP\_t$ )

The  $PVFP$  is derived from projected "Statutory Book Profits," which represent after-tax net income achieved after resetting invested assets to equal statutory reserves. The standard projection formula is:  $$BP\_t \= \\frac{Surplus\_t \- Surplus\_{t-1} \\times (1 \+ i)}{1 \- t}$$

* **$Surplus\_t**$  **:**  Surplus at the end of period  $t$ .  
* **$i**$  **:**  The after-tax rate of return on  **undistributed (self-generated) assets** .  
* **$t**$  **:**  The applicable corporate tax rate.

##### The Cost of Capital (CoC)

The  $CoC$  is an explicit allowance for the "lock-in" effect of capital. Because assets supporting Required Capital earn an after-tax investment return ( $i\_t$ ) that is typically lower than the Risk Discount Rate ( $RDR$ ) required by shareholders, an opportunity cost is created:  $$CoC \= RC\_{t-1} \\times (RDR \- i\_t)$$  The total  $CoC$  is the present value of these annual friction costs over the projection horizon.

##### Projected Cash Flow Items

The VIF projection incorporates all elements of the income statement:

* **Inflows:**  Premiums, investment income (net of default risks), and fee income.  
* **Outflows:**  Claims (mortality/morbidity), surrenders, maturities, and expenses (commissions and maintenance).  
* **Adjustments:**  Taxes and the annual change in statutory reserves.

#### 4\. Methodological Spectrum: TEV, EEV, and MCEV

Embedded Value has evolved from Traditional (TEV) deterministic models toward Market Consistent (MCEV) frameworks to improve transparency and comparability.

* **Traditional Embedded Value (TEV):**  Rooted in the  **ABI Guidance 2001 ("Achieved Profits Method")** , TEV uses deterministic projections and implicitly handles risk through a single Risk Discount Rate (RDR). It remains the dominant standard in Asia-Pacific due to its alignment with local pricing models.  
* **European Embedded Value (EEV):**  Introduced by the CFO Forum in  **May 2004** , EEV enhanced TEV by mandating explicit allowances for options and guarantees and expanded risk disclosures.  
* **Market Consistent Embedded Value (MCEV):**  Introduced in  **June 2008** , MCEV is a risk-neutral framework. It values cash flows consistently with market prices, using asset return simulations that do not capitalise a risk premium.

#### 5\. Standardisation of Actuarial Assumptions

Integrity in EV calculations requires a "best estimate" philosophy: assumptions must be free of Provisions for Adverse Deviation (PADs) to ensure a realistic view of value.

##### Setting Non-Economic Assumptions

* **Mortality/Morbidity:**  Calibrated using company-specific experience against industry tables. Projections should include future improvements for long-duration risks (e.g., annuities).  
* **Persistency:**  Lapse and surrender rates must reflect the dynamic relationship between policyholder behavior and the economic environment.  
* **Expenses:**  Allocated between acquisition, overhead, and maintenance. Crucially, future "efficiency gains" are typically excluded. For example, in  **India** , the benefit of future expense efficiencies is explicitly disallowed to prevent over-optimistic valuations.

##### Derivation of the Risk Discount Rate (RDR)

The RDR is most frequently derived using the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM):  $$e \= RF \+ \\beta \\times (RM \- RF)$$  Where  **$RF**$  is the risk-free rate,  **$\\beta**$  is the risk factor, and  **$(RM \- RF)**$  is the market equity risk premium.While a "Top-down" approach uses a group-level WACC, a  **"Bottom-up"**  approach uses product-specific betas. These betas are not merely market betas; they reflect the expected  **volatility of product cash flows**  relative to assets.

#### 6\. Advanced Valuation: TVFOG and Risk Management

Deterministic projections often fail to capture the cost of asymmetric risks, addressed via the  **Time Value of Financial Options and Guarantees (TVFOG)** .TVFOG is the difference between the mean value of stochastic runs and the value under a single deterministic scenario.

* **Methodology:**  Simple options (e.g., GMAB riders) may be valued using closed-form solutions like the  **Black-Scholes model** . Complex life policies with multiple interdependencies require full  **Stochastic modeling** .  
* **Hybrid Approach Controversy:**  A "Hybrid Approach" uses deterministic real-world best estimates for basic EV and a risk-neutral framework for TVFOG. While practitioners argue this captures hedging costs accurately, the  **CFO Forum initially rejected the hybrid approach**  for EEV, preferring full consistency within a chosen framework.

#### 7\. Analysis of Movement and Sensitivity Testing

The  **Analysis of Movement**  is the ultimate management tool for explaining value changes over a period.

* **Value of New Business (VNB):**  Value added by new sales.  
* **In-force Performance:**  Includes "Expected Return," operating variances (actual vs. expected experience), and operating assumption changes.  
* **Economic Variances:**  Impact of investment performance and economic assumption shifts.The  **Expected Return**  on existing business is:  $$Exp. Return \= (FS\_{t-1} \\times i) \+ (PVDE\_{t-1} \\times RDR)$$  Here,  $PVDE$  represents the  **Value of In-Force including Required Capital** .

##### Mandatory Sensitivity Tests

The CFO Forum mandates testing to highlight volatility, including:

* 100bps shift in RDR or the interest rate environment.  
* 10% change in equity/property values or lapse/expense rates.  
* 5% change in mortality/morbidity.

#### 8\. Strategic Reporting and the IFRS 17 Paradigm

IFRS 17 and EV coexist but serve different masters. While EV measures shareholder value, IFRS 17 measures financial position and profit emergence.

##### The Bridge: CSM vs. VIF

The  **Contractual Service Margin (CSM)**  is an  **"unearned shareholder profit"**  (a liability), whereas the  **Value In-Force (VIF)**  is a metric representing  **"future expected shareholder cash flows"**  (an asset-like measure).| Feature | Traditional EV (TEV) | IFRS 17 || \------ | \------ | \------ || **Contract Boundaries** | Judgmental; may include renewals. | Strictly defined; limited to substantive rights. || **Expenses** | Fully attributable (all management costs). | Only directly attributable expenses included. || **Discount Rate** | Often prescriptive/Top-down. | Principle-based; Top-down or Bottom-up. |

##### Market Perception and P/EV Multiples

While many mature insurers trade at Price-to-EV (P/EV) multiples below 1.0x due to regulatory capital "mark-downs," there are significant outliers.  **AIA and several insurers in India (e.g., HDFC Life, SBI Life, ICICI Prudential)**  consistently trade at multiples significantly  **above 1.0x** .Trading volumes also drive variance: the P/EV ratios of actively traded stocks like  **AIA and Prudential**  show high sensitivity to external events, whereas lower-volume stocks like  **GEH (Great Eastern Holdings)**  often trade at deeper discounts to EV. Despite new IFRS 17 metrics, EV remains the cornerstone for long-term value assessment and M\&A transactions.
